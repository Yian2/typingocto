10 Typing Practice Exercises to Improve Speed and Accuracy

Typing is a skill most people use every day, whether they're writing emails, preparing documents, studying or chatting online. However, typing frequently doesn't always mean typing efficiently.

If you often look down at your keyboard, make spelling mistakes or struggle with numbers and punctuation, a few targeted typing practice exercises can help you improve.

The key is to practise with a clear goal instead of simply typing random words as quickly as possible.

In this guide, you'll discover 10 typing practice exercises designed to help beginners and adults develop better speed, accuracy and keyboard confidence.

You can complete most of these exercises in just a few minutes and gradually increase the difficulty as your skills improve.

Why Are Typing Practice Exercises Important?

Typing practice helps develop muscle memory, improve finger coordination and make keyboard movements more familiar.

When you first learn to type, you consciously think about where each letter is located. With consistent practice, you can begin finding keys more automatically.

This is the foundation of touch typing: using the keyboard without constantly looking at your hands.

Regular practice can help you:

  • Reduce common typing mistakes.
  • Become more comfortable with the keyboard.
  • Improve typing accuracy.
  • Develop a consistent typing rhythm.
  • Increase your words per minute (WPM).
  • Spend less time correcting text.
  • Build confidence when writing longer documents.

However, speed should not be your only objective.

Accurate typing is more useful than fast typing filled with mistakes.

If you're just starting, explore the free typing lessons on TypingOcto before attempting more advanced exercises.

1. Practise the Home Row Keys

The home row is the starting point for learning touch typing.

On a standard English QWERTY keyboard, place your fingers on these keys:

  • Left pinky: A
  • Left ring finger: S
  • Left middle finger: D
  • Left index finger: F
  • Right index finger: J
  • Right middle finger: K
  • Right ring finger: L
  • Right pinky: ;

Your thumbs should rest comfortably near the space bar.

Home Row Exercise

Type the following combinations slowly:

asdf jkl;

ffff jjjj

asdf asdf jkl; jkl;

fj dk sl as fj dk sl as

Repeat each line until you can complete it without frequently looking down.

What Should You Focus On?

Don't worry about typing quickly.

Pay attention to finger placement, consistent movements and returning your fingers to their starting positions.

Practice time: 2–3 minutes.

2. Train Your Fingers With Common English Words

Once you're comfortable with individual letters, start practising complete words.

Common words are useful because they contain letter combinations you encounter frequently when reading and writing English.

Word Practice Exercise

Type these words repeatedly:

the and you that with

have from this your they

people about would there which

Next, practise short sentences:

"The people are working together."

"You can improve with regular practice."

"This exercise will help your typing."

Why This Exercise Helps

Repeatedly typing familiar words allows you to concentrate on your finger movements rather than thinking about complicated vocabulary.

Gradually increase your speed while maintaining good accuracy.

Practice time: 3 minutes.

3. Practise Typing Without Looking at the Keyboard

One of the most valuable typing skills is finding keys without looking down.

This may feel difficult initially, especially if you've spent years using only a few fingers.

Start with a small group of keys and gradually expand.

No-Looking Exercise

  1. Place your fingers on the home row.
  2. Look at your screen instead of your hands.
  3. Type a short sentence.
  4. Correct mistakes when necessary.
  5. Repeat the sentence until your movements feel more comfortable.

Try this sentence:

"Practice makes progress when you stay consistent."

Don't rush.

If you make a mistake, pause and find the correct key using your finger positioning.

Over time, your fingers should become more familiar with the keyboard layout.

Typing tip: If you constantly look down, reduce your speed and practise shorter sequences before moving on to longer sentences.

4. Improve Accuracy With Difficult Letter Combinations

Some letter combinations are more challenging than others because they require quick movements between different fingers.

Practising these sequences separately can help improve coordination.

Letter Combination Exercise

Try these combinations:

th th th th

ing ing ing

tion tion tion

ough ough ough

str str str

Then practise complete words:

  • thinking
  • strength
  • through
  • communication
  • interesting
  • understanding

How to Improve

Notice which combinations cause the most mistakes.

Instead of repeating the entire exercise, spend extra time practising the difficult sequence.

For example, if you often mistype "strength", practise "str" before attempting the complete word again.

Practice time: 3–4 minutes.

5. Practise Numbers and Everyday Data

Typing numbers is especially useful for students, office workers and people who regularly work with spreadsheets.

Many people become comfortable typing letters but struggle when entering dates, quantities or numerical information.

Number Typing Exercise

Practise these sequences using the number row:

12345 67890

2026 2027 2028

15 25 35 45 55

Then try realistic examples:

Order number: 48291

Invoice total: 125.50

Delivery date: 20/11/2026

Why Number Practice Matters

Numbers often require different finger movements from regular text.

Practising them helps you become more comfortable switching between words, digits and symbols.

This can be particularly helpful when entering information into forms or spreadsheets.

Practice time: 2–3 minutes.

6. Master Punctuation and Special Characters

Being able to type letters quickly is only part of becoming a confident typist.

Everyday writing also includes commas, periods, apostrophes, question marks and other punctuation.

These characters can interrupt your rhythm if you're unfamiliar with their positions.

Punctuation Exercise

Practise these sentences:

"Hello, how are you today?"

"I can't believe it's already Friday!"

"Please send the document by 3:30 PM."

"Typing is useful: it saves time and reduces effort."

Focus on using the correct fingers and maintaining a comfortable rhythm.

Remember that symbol locations can vary depending on your keyboard layout.

Practice time: 3 minutes.

7. Simulate Real Office Typing

A practical way to improve typing is to practise the type of text you encounter in everyday situations.

If you use a computer for work, try typing short professional emails and messages.

Email Typing Exercise

Type the following example:

"Good morning,

Thank you for your message.

I have reviewed the information and will send the updated document this afternoon.

Please let me know if you need anything else.

Best regards"

This exercise combines:

  • Capital letters.
  • Punctuation.
  • Short paragraphs.
  • Common professional vocabulary.
  • Line breaks.
  • Natural sentence structures.

Why Realistic Practice Works

Realistic writing exercises help you become comfortable with the keyboard movements required for everyday communication.

They can also reveal weaknesses that simple word repetition doesn't expose.

Practice time: 4–5 minutes.

8. Use a Typing Speed Test to Measure Progress

Typing practice becomes more useful when you measure your progress over time.

A typing speed test can help you understand how quickly and accurately you type.

Two important measurements are:

Words Per Minute (WPM)

WPM measures your typing speed.

Many typing tests use a standard calculation based on five characters per word, although individual platforms may use slightly different methods.

Typing Accuracy

Accuracy measures how closely your typed text matches the expected text.

For example, two people might achieve similar WPM scores while making very different numbers of mistakes.

Speed Test Exercise

  1. Open a free typing speed test.
  2. Choose a consistent test duration.
  3. Complete the test without rushing.
  4. Record your WPM and accuracy.
  5. Repeat the exercise after several practice sessions.

Avoid comparing results from tests with very different lengths or text difficulty.

The goal is to measure your own improvement.

9. Challenge Yourself With Longer Paragraphs

Once short typing exercises feel comfortable, move on to longer paragraphs.

Longer texts help you practise maintaining a steady rhythm over time.

Paragraph Typing Exercise

Type the following paragraph:

"Learning to type efficiently is a useful skill in a world where computers are part of everyday life. Whether you are preparing a school assignment, writing an email or working on a project, accurate typing can make your tasks easier. The best way to improve is to practise regularly, focus on your mistakes and gradually increase your speed."

What Should You Focus On?

Try to maintain consistent accuracy throughout the entire paragraph.

Notice whether you start making more mistakes as the exercise continues.

If your accuracy drops, take a short break and return to a comfortable pace.

Practice time: 4 minutes.

10. Create a Daily Typing Practice Routine

You don't need an extremely long practice session to work on your typing skills.

A short routine can help you build consistency without feeling overwhelmed.

15-Minute Typing Practice Plan

Try this daily schedule:

  • Minutes 1–3: Home row and finger placement.
  • Minutes 4–6: Common words and difficult combinations.
  • Minutes 7–9: Numbers and punctuation.
  • Minutes 10–13: Complete sentences or paragraphs.
  • Minutes 14–15: Typing speed test.

Record your results after each session.

You can follow the routine for one week, then adjust the exercises based on the mistakes you make most often.

If you struggle with punctuation, dedicate additional time to symbols.

If your accuracy is already good, gradually increase the difficulty of your typing tests.

Practice tip: Consistency matters more than achieving a perfect score in every session. Focus on gradual improvement rather than comparing yourself with experienced typists.

How Often Should You Practise Typing?

The best practice schedule is one you can maintain.

For many beginners, starting with 10–15 minutes a day is a manageable approach.

You can increase the duration as your concentration and comfort improve.

However, practising for longer doesn't automatically guarantee better results.

Pay attention to fatigue, posture and accuracy.

If you notice that you're making more mistakes than usual, a short break may be more helpful than continuing to type.

Common Mistakes to Avoid During Typing Practice

Even regular practice can become less effective when you develop unhelpful habits.

Trying to Type Too Fast

Speed is important, but pushing yourself too hard can increase errors.

Start slowly and raise your speed when your accuracy becomes consistent.

Looking at Your Hands Constantly

Looking down can make it harder to develop touch typing.

Begin with familiar keys and gradually practise without watching your fingers.

Ignoring Difficult Keys

It's tempting to repeat exercises that feel easy.

However, spending time on difficult keys and combinations can help address weaknesses.

Practising Without Measuring Progress

Without tracking your results, it can be difficult to notice improvements.

Use regular tests to compare your accuracy and speed.

Forgetting to Take Breaks

Typing should feel comfortable.

Maintain a relaxed posture, avoid unnecessary tension and take breaks during longer sessions.

Frequently Asked Questions About Typing Practice

What Are the Best Typing Practice Exercises for Beginners?

Home row drills, common-word repetition, short sentences and beginner typing lessons are useful starting points. They allow you to develop familiar finger movements before attempting complex text.

Can Typing Practice Improve My WPM?

Regular, focused practice can help improve typing speed over time. The results depend on your starting ability, consistency and the exercises you choose.

Is 15 Minutes of Typing Practice a Day Enough?

Fifteen minutes is a reasonable starting routine for many learners. Focus on accuracy, gradually increase difficulty and adapt your practice time to your progress.

Should I Focus on Typing Speed or Accuracy First?

Beginners often benefit from prioritising accuracy. Once you can type consistently with fewer mistakes, gradually work on increasing speed.
