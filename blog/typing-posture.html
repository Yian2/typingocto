Best Typing Posture: How to Sit and Avoid Hand Pain

By TypingOcto Team·Ergonomics·November 13, 2026·3 min read

Your posture affects how fast and how long you can type. A few small changes to your chair, desk and hands can make typing more comfortable and help you avoid tension.

Your chair and feet

  • Sit all the way back in your chair with your back supported.
  • Keep your feet flat on the floor or on a footrest.
  • Your knees should be roughly level with your hips.

Your screen

  • Place the top of the screen at about eye level.
  • Keep it roughly an arm's length away.
  • If you use a laptop for long periods, consider a stand and an external keyboard.

Your arms and wrists

  • Relax your shoulders and keep your elbows close to your body, bent at about 90 degrees.
  • Keep your wrists straight, not bent up or down.
  • Let your wrists float while typing; use a wrist rest only during pauses.

Your hands and fingers

Curve your fingers gently over the home row and press the keys lightly. You don't need to hit the keys hard. Lighter typing is faster and less tiring.

💡 If you notice your shoulders rising towards your ears, stop, breathe out and drop them. Tension slows your fingers down.

Take short breaks

Every 30 minutes or so, stand up, move around and look at something far away to rest your eyes. Short breaks help you stay focused and comfortable.

Simple hand stretches

  • Finger spread: open your hands wide, hold for 5 seconds, then make a gentle fist. Repeat 5 times.
  • Wrist circles: slowly rotate your wrists in both directions.
  • Prayer stretch: press your palms together in front of your chest and gently lower your hands.

Stretches should never hurt. If you have ongoing pain, numbness or tingling in your hands or wrists, talk to a doctor or physiotherapist.

Put it into practice

Set up your desk, then try a relaxed practice session focusing only on staying loose. You may notice that your accuracy goes up when your hands are relaxed. Then read how to type faster.

Frequently asked questions

Should my wrists rest on the desk while typing?

It is better to let your wrists float while typing and rest them only during pauses. Keep them straight rather than bent.

How often should I take a break from typing?

A short break every 30 minutes or so helps your hands, eyes and focus.
