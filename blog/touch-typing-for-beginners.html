Touch Typing for Beginners: The Complete Guide

By TypingOcto Team·Touch typing basics·November 7, 2026·3 min read

Touch typing means typing with all ten fingers, without looking at the keyboard. It is one of the most useful skills you can learn for school and work, and anyone can learn it.

What is touch typing?

With touch typing, every finger has its own set of keys, and you find them by feel instead of by sight. Your eyes stay on the screen, so you can read what you write and catch mistakes immediately. The opposite is "hunt and peck": looking for each key and pressing it with one or two fingers.

Why learn it?

  • Speed: touch typists are usually much faster than two-finger typists.
  • Focus: you think about what you are writing, not where the keys are.
  • Comfort: using all fingers spreads the work and reduces strain.
  • Confidence: exams, homework and job applications all involve typing.

Step 1: Find the home row

Put your left fingers on A S D F and right fingers on J K L ;. Feel the small bumps on F and J with your index fingers. Your thumbs rest on the space bar. This is your starting position, and you return to it after every key. More details in our home row guide.

Step 2: Learn which finger presses which key

Each finger moves up and down in a slanted column:

  • Left pinky: Q, A, Z (and 1)
  • Left ring: W, S, X (and 2)
  • Left middle: E, D, C (and 3)
  • Left index: R, F, V, T, G, B (and 4, 5)
  • Right index: Y, H, N, U, J, M (and 6, 7)
  • Right middle: I, K, comma (and 8)
  • Right ring: O, L, period (and 9)
  • Right pinky: P, ;, ', / (and 0)
  • Thumbs: space bar

On TypingOcto the virtual keyboard uses these same colours, and it tells you the right finger for the next key.

Step 3: Add a few keys at a time

Don't try to learn the whole keyboard at once. Start with the home row, then add the top row, then the bottom row, then capitals, numbers and punctuation. Our 100 lessons follow exactly this order.

Step 4: Don't look down

This is the hardest part. If you really need to, peek at the on-screen keyboard instead of your hands. Some people cover their hands with a light cloth for the first week.

Step 5: Practise daily and track progress

Practise 10–15 minutes a day. After the basic lessons, take a typing test every few days to measure your speed. If you want to know what to aim for, read how long it takes to learn touch typing.

💡 Expect to feel slower than before for a week or two. That is normal: you are replacing an old habit with a better one.

Frequently asked questions

Can adults learn touch typing?

Absolutely. Adults often learn quickly because they are motivated and already know the keyboard layout. Daily practice is the key.

Which keys should I learn first?

Start with the home row: A S D F and J K L ;. Then add G and H, followed by the top row and the bottom row.

Is touch typing hard?

It feels awkward for the first few days, but it becomes natural with practice. Most beginners are comfortable within a few weeks.
