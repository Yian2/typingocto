Typing for Kids: How to Make Learning Fun

By TypingOcto Team·Kids & schools·November 11, 2026·3 min read

Kids use computers and tablets every day, but very few learn to type properly. The good news: with short sessions and a bit of fun, children pick up touch typing surprisingly fast.

What age should kids start typing?

There is no single right age, but many children are ready to start touch typing once their hands are big enough to rest on the home row comfortably and they can read simple words, often around 7 or 8 years old. Younger children can still explore the keyboard and learn where letters are.

Keep sessions short

Children learn best in short bursts of 5 to 15 minutes. Stop while it is still fun, not when they are frustrated. One lesson per day is a perfect routine.

Turn it into a game

  • Celebrate stars and new levels.
  • Join the weekly challenge to win an exclusive hat.
  • Compare scores with brothers, sisters or classmates, and share result cards.

Focus on good habits, not speed

At the start, the goal is to use the right fingers and not look down. Praise accuracy more than speed. Speed will come later, and it will come faster if the basics are solid.

💡 A sticker on the F and J keys can help small hands find the home row.

Set up a comfortable space

Many desks and chairs are designed for adults. Make sure your child's feet are supported (a box or a few books work), the screen is at eye level and the keyboard is close enough that their elbows stay near their body. See our guide on typing posture.

Tips for teachers

  • Start each computer class with a 5-minute typing warm-up.
  • Use the weekly challenge as a friendly class competition.
  • TypingOcto needs no accounts or emails, which makes it easy to use in class.

How long should kids practise typing each day?

About 5 to 15 minutes a day is ideal. Short, regular sessions keep it fun and build muscle memory.
