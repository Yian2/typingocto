How to Improve Typing Accuracy: 7 Exercises to Make Fewer Mistakes

Typing faster is a common goal, but speed means very little if you constantly have to stop and correct mistakes. Whether you're a student, a professional, or someone learning touch typing for the first time, improving your typing accuracy can make everyday computer tasks easier and more efficient.

The good news? You don't need to spend hours practising. With the right exercises and a consistent routine, you can gradually build muscle memory, reduce typing errors, and become more confident at the keyboard.

In this guide, you'll discover seven practical exercises to improve your typing accuracy and learn how to measure your progress.

What Is Typing Accuracy?

Typing accuracy measures how correctly you type a given text. It is usually expressed as a percentage.

For example, if you type 100 characters and make 5 errors, your accuracy would be approximately 95%, assuming each character is counted once.

Here's a simple reference table:

Typing AccuracyGeneral Interpretation
Below 90%Focus on reducing mistakes
90–94%Developing consistency
95–97%Good accuracy for practice
98–99%Excellent accuracy
100%Perfect accuracy on that test

These are practical training guidelines, not universal industry standards. Different typing tests may calculate accuracy differently.

For beginners, aiming for 95% accuracy before increasing speed can be a useful starting point.

Why Does Typing Accuracy Matter?

High typing accuracy helps you work more smoothly and spend less time correcting what you've written.

When you make frequent mistakes, your typing rhythm gets interrupted. You may need to stop, find the incorrect letter, press Backspace, and type the word again.

This can make a high words-per-minute (WPM) score less useful than it initially appears.

Improving accuracy also helps you develop reliable finger movements and build confidence when typing without looking at the keyboard.

1. Start Slowly and Focus on Every Keystroke

One of the most common beginner mistakes is trying to type as fast as possible.

Instead, slow down deliberately.

Choose a short typing exercise and focus on pressing the correct keys rather than finishing quickly.

Try This Exercise

  1. Open a beginner typing lesson.
  2. Type for two minutes at a comfortable speed.
  3. Focus on accuracy rather than WPM.
  4. Review your mistakes.
  5. Repeat the lesson, trying to make fewer errors.

Once you can type comfortably and consistently, gradually increase your speed.

2. Master the Home Row Keys

The home row is the foundation of touch typing.

On a standard QWERTY keyboard, the main home row positions are:

  • Left hand: A, S, D, F
  • Right hand: J, K, L, ;

Your index fingers rest on F and J, which usually have small raised marks that help you locate them without looking.

Home Row Practice

Practise these combinations:

asdf jkl;

fj fj dk dk sl sl

asdf jkl; asdf jkl;

Start slowly and keep your fingers close to their starting positions.

The goal is to build consistent movements so you can reach other keys and return to the home row naturally.

3. Practise Common Letter Combinations

Many typing mistakes happen when your fingers have to move quickly between several keys.

Practising common letter combinations can help those movements become more familiar.

Letter Combination Exercise

Try typing:

th th th

er er er

ing ing ing

tion tion tion

Then move on to short words:

  • the there their
  • right light night
  • writing reading learning

Repeat each sequence several times, paying attention to which letters cause mistakes.

If you consistently mistype a particular combination, practise it separately before returning to full sentences.

4. Stop Looking at Your Keyboard

Looking down at the keyboard may feel helpful, but it can interrupt the development of touch typing skills.

Try a short exercise without looking at your hands.

Begin with familiar keys, such as F and J, and gradually add more letters.

If you make a mistake, pause and identify the correct finger rather than immediately looking down.

You don't need to memorise the entire keyboard in one session.

Start with the home row, then work towards the upper and lower rows.

Over time, repeated practice will help your fingers locate the keys with less conscious effort.

5. Practise Difficult Words Separately

Not every word is equally easy to type.

Long words, repeated letters, punctuation and unfamiliar combinations can increase your error rate.

Create a small personal list of words you frequently mistype.

Difficult Word Exercise

For example:

  • necessary
  • different
  • successful
  • experience
  • communication

Type each word five times slowly, then practise it in a sentence.

Example:

"Clear communication is necessary for successful teamwork."

This approach helps you target specific weaknesses instead of repeating only the words you already type correctly.

6. Use Short Typing Tests to Track Your Progress

Typing tests are useful for measuring improvement, but they shouldn't become a race to achieve the highest possible WPM.

When you complete a test, pay attention to two measurements:

  • Words per minute (WPM): how quickly you type.
  • Typing accuracy: how correctly you reproduce the text.

Example of Typing Progress

TestSpeedAccuracy
Test 145 WPM89%
Test 239 WPM98%
Test 343 WPM98%

These are hypothetical results.

The second test demonstrates better accuracy, while the third suggests that speed has improved without sacrificing precision.

To measure your progress fairly, use the same test duration and similar text difficulty.

7. Follow a 10-Minute Daily Typing Routine

Consistency is more useful than occasionally practising for a very long session.

You can start with this simple routine:

DurationActivity
2 minutesHome row warm-up
3 minutesBeginner or intermediate typing lesson
2 minutesPractise difficult words
2 minutesTyping accuracy exercise
1 minuteTyping speed test

Try following this routine for one week.

Keep track of your results and note which keys or combinations give you the most trouble.

If your accuracy drops when you try to increase speed, slow down and focus on building consistency again.

Common Typing Mistakes to Avoid

Even with regular practice, certain habits can slow down your improvement.

Prioritising Speed Over Accuracy

Typing faster isn't always better. Try to develop accurate movements before pushing your speed.

Using the Wrong Fingers

Inconsistent finger placement can make touch typing more difficult to learn. Use a consistent finger-to-key technique while practising.

Practising Only Easy Words

You improve by working on the combinations that cause problems, not just repeating familiar text.

Ignoring Your Posture

Sit comfortably, keep your shoulders relaxed and avoid bending your wrists into uncomfortable positions. Take breaks when needed.

Practising Without Tracking Results

Recording your accuracy and WPM helps you understand whether you're improving.

How Long Does It Take to Improve Typing Accuracy?

There is no universal timeline.

Your progress depends on your starting skill level, practice frequency, keyboard familiarity and the types of exercises you use.

Some learners notice fewer mistakes after several consistent practice sessions. Developing reliable touch typing across the entire keyboard generally requires more sustained practice.

Instead of comparing yourself with other people, focus on your own results over time.

Even small improvements are meaningful.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Is a Good Typing Accuracy Percentage?

A practical goal for many learners is 95% accuracy or higher. As you become more comfortable, you can aim for 98–99%, depending on the test and your needs.

Is Typing Accuracy More Important Than Speed?

Both matter, but beginners often benefit from prioritising accuracy. Increasing speed becomes more useful when you can maintain consistent, correct keystrokes.

Can I Improve Typing Accuracy Without Looking at the Keyboard?

Yes. Progressive touch typing exercises help develop finger familiarity and reduce the need to look down. Start with a small set of keys and gradually expand.

Should I Practise Typing Every Day?

Short, regular sessions are a practical way to build consistency. Even ten minutes of focused practice can help you develop a sustainable learning habit.

Why Does My Typing Accuracy Decrease When I Type Faster?

When you try to move faster than your current level of finger control allows, you're more likely to press incorrect keys. Reduce your speed temporarily and increase it gradually.

Start Improving Your Typing Accuracy Today

Better typing accuracy doesn't come from trying to break your speed record every time you sit at a keyboard.

It comes from patient practice, consistent technique and paying attention to your mistakes.

Start with the home row, work on difficult combinations, and track your progress with short typing tests.
