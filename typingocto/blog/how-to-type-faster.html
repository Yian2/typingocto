How to Type Faster: 10 Tips That Actually Work

By TypingOcto Team·Typing speed·November 3, 2026·3 min read

Typing faster is not about moving your fingers like crazy. It is about building good habits, so your hands know where to go without thinking. Here are ten tips that make a real difference.

1. Learn touch typing (stop looking at the keyboard)

The single biggest jump in speed comes from touch typing: typing with all ten fingers without looking down. Every time your eyes travel from the screen to the keys and back, you lose time and focus. It feels slower for the first few days, but it is the only way to reach 60+ WPM. Our free typing lessons teach it step by step, starting from the home row.

2. Always start from the home row

Place your left fingers on A S D F and your right fingers on J K L ;. Your index fingers should feel the small bumps on F and J. After every word, your fingers return to this position. This "home base" is what lets you find every other key without looking. Read more in our guide to the home row keys.

3. Use the right finger for every key

Each finger is responsible for its own column of keys. It is tempting to use your strongest fingers for everything, but that creates long, slow movements. On TypingOcto the keyboard is colour-coded, so you always see which finger should press the next key.

4. Accuracy first, speed second

This sounds backwards, but it works. When you type fast and make mistakes, you spend extra time fixing them, and your brain "learns" the wrong movement. Aim for 95% accuracy or more. Once your fingers move correctly, speed comes naturally.

💡 Rule of thumb: if your accuracy drops below 92%, slow down for a few minutes. If it stays above 97%, push your speed a little.

5. Practise a little every day

Ten minutes every day beats one hour once a week. Short, regular sessions help your muscle memory grow without getting tired. That is exactly why Inky, our octopus, gets hungry if you don't come back for a few days.

6. Keep a steady rhythm

Try to press keys at an even pace, like a metronome, instead of typing fast bursts followed by pauses. A steady rhythm reduces errors and is less tiring. Some people like typing along to calm music.

7. Think in words, not letters

Beginners read one letter at a time. Advanced typists read and type whole words, especially common ones like the, and, that and with. Practise the most common English words until they flow automatically.

8. Train your weak keys

Most people have a few keys that slow them down, often Q, Z, X, P or the number row. Our progress page shows an error heatmap of your keyboard, and the Weak keys practice mode builds words with exactly the letters you miss most.

9. Sit well and relax your hands

Good posture helps you type longer and faster: feet flat on the floor, screen at eye level, elbows at about 90 degrees and wrists floating above the keyboard. Tense shoulders make your fingers slower. See our full guide to typing posture.

10. Measure your progress

Take a typing speed test once or twice a week and write down your WPM. Seeing the number go up is a great motivator. On TypingOcto your results are saved automatically and shown in a chart.

How long will it take?

With 10 to 15 minutes of daily practice, many people go from hunt-and-peck to touch typing in a few weeks, and keep improving after that. Everyone is different, so focus on your own progress rather than comparing yourself to others. Curious about the numbers? Read what a good typing speed is.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to improve typing speed?

Learn touch typing with the correct fingers, focus on accuracy first and practise 10–15 minutes every day. Most people see clear progress within a few weeks.

Should I look at the keyboard while typing?

No. Looking down slows you down. At first it feels hard, but after a few days of practice your fingers learn where every key is.

Is 10 minutes a day enough to type faster?

Yes. Short daily sessions are more effective than long sessions once a week because muscle memory builds with repetition.
